# Safety matrix

One row per safety rule: what it says in plain words, where the rule came from, where it lives in the code, which tests pin it, and one before and after example. The golden cases are in `data/eval/golden_cases.json` and run with `python tasks.py eval-fast` (results: `docs/RESULTS.md`). Where a rule has no golden case, the example is from the named unit test, and the row says so.

**Read this first.**

- **No agronomic or veterinary review.** No vet and no farmer has validated these thresholds (README, "Known limitations"). Only the rows marked "published reference" cite a published finding, and even those cite it for something other than a clinical threshold.
- **General guidance may not fit a given farm.** The last column marks the rows whose numbers are general guidance or the builder's heuristics. A different breed, climate, house or management can make a normal day look like an alert, or an alert look normal. Treat every alert as a reminder to look and to call the vet, not as a diagnosis.
- **CluckTalk never gives a diagnosis, a medicine or a dose.** A suspected notifiable disease gets the fixed steps (isolate, stop bird movement, report to a vet or the animal husbandry department) and no category.
- **Code decides.** The levels, the urgency floor and the vet advice are computed in code from the records and the signs. A model, when live AI is switched on, can raise an urgency and can write no text. Live AI has not been verified end to end here (README claims table).
- Source words used below: **the farmer** (a number or a routine relayed to the builder, not confirmed in writing), **published reference**, **heuristic** (CluckTalk's own early-warning choice, `src/clucktalk/domain/thresholds.py`), **project rule** (a rule of this project, written in `docs/DESIGN.md`), **not recorded**.

## What this matrix does not cover

- It lists the rules a farmer's safety can depend on. The other checks (for example the shed-name reader, the protocol errors and the fuzz tests) are in `docs/DESIGN.md` section 4 and `docs/RESULTS.md`.
- Alexa+ composes its own speech from a tool's result, so a safety step that lives only in our sentence can be left out when Alexa+ speaks. The structured `safety` field exists so a host can show the urgency itself. We do not claim Alexa+ uses it (README).
- Telugu: 86 of 434 strings reviewed by the builder, a native Telugu speaker; 348 pending (including the pilot page); no independent review. (`docs/TELUGU_REVIEW.md`)
- In **pilot mode**, a shed with no alert is described as "no alert from the checks I can make", never as doing well, because the checks are few and the thresholds above are general guidance. In demo mode a shed with no alert is still described as doing well (README, "Known limitations").
